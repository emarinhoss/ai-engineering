A. Compressible flow and heating

White, Viscous Fluid Flow, chapter 7 (compressible boundary layers). Free: NASA Glenn "Oblique shock" and "Normal shock" pages; NACA Report 1135 (tables and formulas).

In the talk: S4. Record: docs/specs/00_decisions.md section 1, physics/atmosphere.py.

Definition

The flight condition is Mach 4.0 at 30 km in the US Standard Atmosphere 1976. The freestream state fixes every other number: static temperature, pressure, speed of sound, viscosity and unit Reynolds number. The wall is isothermal at 300 K. The body is sub-scale, about 0.3 m long.

Key relations
\[ \begin{aligned} a &= \sqrt{\gamma R T}, \qquad U = M a \\ T_0 &= T\left(1 + \tfrac{\gamma-1}{2} M^2\right), \qquad p_0 = p\left(1 + \tfrac{\gamma-1}{2} M^2\right)^{\gamma/(\gamma-1)} = p\left(1 + \tfrac{1}{5} M^2\right)^{7/2} \\ \mu &= \mu_{\mathrm{ref}} \left(\frac{T}{T_{\mathrm{ref}}}\right)^{3/2} \frac{T_{\mathrm{ref}} + S}{T + S} \quad \text{(Sutherland)}, \qquad \frac{Re}{\mathrm{m}} = \frac{\rho U}{\mu} \end{aligned} \]
Worked example (numbers from 00_decisions.md section 1)

With \(T = 226.509\ \mathrm{K}\) and \(p = 1197.03\ \mathrm{Pa}\):

\[ \begin{aligned} a &= \sqrt{1.4 \times 287.058 \times 226.509} = 301.71\ \mathrm{m/s}, \qquad U = 4 \times 301.71 = 1206.8\ \mathrm{m/s} \\ T_0 &= 226.509 \left(1 + \tfrac{16}{5}\right) = 226.509 \times 4.2 = 951.3\ \mathrm{K} \\ p_0 &= 1197.03 \times 4.2^{7/2} = 1.8175 \times 10^{5}\ \mathrm{Pa} \\ \frac{Re}{\mathrm{m}} &= 1.506 \times 10^{6}\ \mathrm{m^{-1}} \;\Rightarrow\; Re_{0.3\,\mathrm{m}} \approx 4.5 \times 10^{5} \end{aligned} \] Interview questions
Why 30 km and not a lower, more realistic altitude?

At 30 km the unit Reynolds number is 1.5e6 per meter, so a 0.3 m body stays below Re 5e5 and the laminar model is defensible. At 25 km the same body would be near transition and a turbulent model would be required. The choice is stated as a modeling choice, not as a mission.

Why is the wall at 300 K when T0 is 951 K?

A cold wall is the normal test-facility condition and it gives a well-posed heat-flux objective. A radiative-equilibrium wall would add an unknown emissivity and a nonlinear boundary condition. The cold wall also thins the boundary layer, which keeps the flow laminar longer.

Self-check
What is the total temperature at Mach 4 if T = 226.5 K?

951 K. Use \(T_0 = T\,(1 + M^2/5)\) with \(M^2 = 16\).

Which function in the repository returns the freestream state?

physics.atmosphere.state(altitude_km). It returns T, p, rho, a and mu for a tabulated altitude.

In the talk: S2 (the terminal shock), S8. Record: physics/shock.py function normal, docs/specs/00_decisions.md section 3.1.

Definition

A normal shock turns supersonic flow into subsonic flow in a very thin layer. Static pressure, density and temperature rise. Total pressure falls because the shock creates entropy. The loss grows fast with the upstream Mach number.

Key relations (calorically perfect gas, gamma = 1.4)
\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{p_2}{p_1} &= 1 + \frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}\left(M_1^2 - 1\right) \\ M_2^2 &= \frac{1 + \frac{\gamma-1}{2} M_1^2}{\gamma M_1^2 - \frac{\gamma-1}{2}} = \frac{1 + \frac{1}{5} M_1^2}{\frac{7}{5} M_1^2 - \frac{1}{5}} \\ \frac{p_{02}}{p_{01}} &= \left[\frac{(\gamma+1) M_1^2}{(\gamma-1) M_1^2 + 2}\right]^{\gamma/(\gamma-1)} \left[\frac{\gamma+1}{2\gamma M_1^2 - (\gamma-1)}\right]^{1/(\gamma-1)} = \left[\frac{6 M_1^2}{M_1^2 + 5}\right]^{7/2} \left[\frac{6}{7 M_1^2 - 1}\right]^{5/2} \end{aligned} \]
Worked example (computed with physics.shock.normal)

From physics.shock.normal:

\[ \left.\frac{p_{02}}{p_{01}}\right|_{M_1 = 4.000} = 0.1388, \qquad \left.\frac{p_{02}}{p_{01}}\right|_{M_1 = 2.962} = 0.3392 \]

\(M_1 = 2.962\) is the inviscid throat Mach number of the 8/8 inlet. The terminal shock at the throat keeps 2.4 times more total pressure than a normal shock in the freestream. This is why the inlet slows the flow with oblique shocks first.

Interview questions
Why does the objective include a normal shock that is not in the CFD?

The inlet feeds a subsonic diffuser, so the flow must pass a terminal shock somewhere. The post-processing takes each streamtube at the throat through a normal shock at its own Mach number. Without this term the best inlet in both objectives is a flat plate, which is not a design.

What is the entropy rise across a shock related to?

To the total pressure ratio: \(\Delta s = -R \ln(p_{02}/p_{01})\). A smaller total pressure ratio means more entropy and more loss.

Self-check
Does total temperature change across an adiabatic shock?

No. Total enthalpy is conserved, so T0 is the same on both sides for a calorically perfect gas.

Order these by total pressure kept at Mach 4: one normal shock, one 16 degree oblique shock plus normal shock, two 8 degree shocks plus normal shock.

\(0.139 \lt 0.288 \lt 0.316\) (all from physics.shock). More, weaker shocks keep more.

In the talk: S6 (V1), S10 (gate 1). Record: runs/task1/v1_oblique_shock.json, physics/shock.py function oblique.

Definition

A wedge of angle theta in supersonic flow makes an oblique shock at angle beta. Only the velocity component normal to the shock, M1 sin(beta), goes through the shock relations. The tangential component does not change. For a given M1 and theta there are two solutions. The weak solution is the one that occurs on an isolated wedge.

Key relation
\[ \begin{aligned} \tan\theta &= 2\cot\beta\,\frac{M_1^2 \sin^2\beta - 1}{M_1^2(\gamma + \cos 2\beta) + 2} \\ M_{n1} &= M_1 \sin\beta, \qquad M_2 = \frac{M_{n2}}{\sin(\beta - \theta)} \end{aligned} \]
Worked example (physics.shock.oblique and the V1 record)

At \(M_1 = 4.0\) and \(\theta = 8^\circ\) the weak solution of the \(\theta\)-\(\beta\)-\(M\) relation is

\[ \beta = 20.47^\circ, \qquad M_{n1} = 4.0 \sin 20.47^\circ = 1.399, \qquad \frac{p_2}{p_1} = 2.117, \qquad M_2 = 3.427, \qquad \frac{p_{02}}{p_{01}} = 0.9584 . \]

The Euler wedge run on Colab (V1) gave \(\Delta\beta = +0.132^\circ\), \(\Delta(p_2/p_1) = -0.12\ \%\) and \(\Delta M_2 = -0.19\ \%\) against the hard tolerances \(0.5^\circ\), \(2\ \%\) and \(2\ \%\).

The maximum deflection at Mach 4 is \(\theta_{\max}(4.0) = 38.77^\circ\). Gate 1 requires \(\theta_1 \le \theta_{\max}(M_\infty) - 2^\circ\) and \(\theta_2 \le \theta_{\max}(M_1) - 2^\circ\), so the shocks stay attached.

Interview questions
What happens when theta is larger than theta_max?

The shock detaches and becomes a curved bow shock ahead of the wedge. The flow behind the central part is subsonic and the total pressure loss is close to a normal shock. The gates refuse such designs before any mesh is built.

Why validate against an oblique shock first?

It is an exact inviscid solution that tests the solver setup, the freestream, the mesh orientation and the units in one short run. A failure there is a setup bug, and no later result can be trusted until it passes.

Self-check
Which Mach number goes into the shock jump relations for an oblique shock?

The normal component \(M_1 \sin\beta\).

Give beta, M2 and p0 ratio for M = 4, theta = 8 degrees.

\(\beta = 20.47^\circ\), \(M_2 = 3.427\), \(p_{02}/p_{01} = 0.9584\).

Definition

Two ramps make two oblique shocks before the terminal normal shock. Each oblique shock removes less total pressure than one strong shock. Oswatitsch showed that, for a fixed number of oblique shocks, the total pressure recovery is a maximum when all oblique shocks have the same normal Mach number, M1 sin(beta1) = M2 sin(beta2).

Interview questions
Why not add a third ramp?

More ramps give more recovery, but the design space would have three variables, the body would be longer and the heat load would rise. The study keeps two variables so the Pareto front can be shown on a plane and every point can be checked by hand.

Is your design on the Oswatitsch optimum?

Not by construction. The Oswatitsch condition is near theta2 = theta1 + 2 degrees in this box, and the design map shows the GP mean along that cut against the analytic curve. The Pareto front is defined by two objectives, so its points need not sit on the single-objective optimum.

Self-check
Why is the second shock angle given relative to the first ramp?

The flow after shock 1 is parallel to ramp 1. The second shock turns it by a further theta2, so its angle is measured from the ramp 1 direction.

State the Oswatitsch condition in one sentence.

Equal normal Mach numbers on all oblique shocks give the maximum recovery for that number of shocks.

Definition

recovery_total is the mass-flux-weighted mean of local total pressure at the throat plane, after each streamtube passes a normal shock at its own Mach number, divided by the freestream total pressure. recovery_throat is the same quantity without the terminal shock. The cowl lip is placed where the two oblique shocks meet (shock on lip), so the capture is full at the design Mach number and the cowl position is not a design variable.

Key relation
\[ \begin{aligned} \mathrm{recovery\_total} &= \frac{\sum_i w_i\,\phi_i\,p_{0,i}\,\pi_{NS}(M_i)}{\sum_i w_i\,\phi_i}\;\frac{1}{p_{0,\infty}} \\ \phi_i &= \rho_i\left(u_i n_x + v_i n_y\right), \qquad p_{0,i} = p_i\left(1 + \tfrac{1}{5} M_i^2\right)^{7/2}, \qquad \pi_{NS}(M) = 1 \;\text{for}\; M \le 1 \end{aligned} \]
Worked example (pool record inlet2r-fd8bbe1147, runs/pool)

Design \(\theta_1 = 10.84^\circ\), \(\theta_2 = 10.20^\circ\):

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{inviscid two-shock: } & \frac{p_{0,\mathrm{th}}}{p_{0,\infty}} = 0.8664, \quad M_{\mathrm{th}} = 2.677 \\ \text{CFD (laminar): } & \mathrm{recovery\_throat} = 0.8530, \quad \bar{M}_{\mathrm{th}} = 2.627, \quad \mathrm{recovery\_total} = 0.3747 \end{aligned} \]

The boundary layer costs \(0.8664 - 0.8530 = 0.0134\) (\(1.5\ \%\)) of total pressure before the throat. The cross-check against SU2's own AVG_TOTALPRESS passed (X1, \(0.5\ \%\)).

Interview questions
Why weight by mass flux and not by area?

The engine receives mass, not area. A mass-flux weight counts each streamtube by what it delivers. Area weighting would over-count the slow boundary-layer fluid near the walls.

What about off-design Mach numbers?

Below Mach 4 the shocks move ahead of the lip and the inlet spills; above it they enter the duct. The study is at one design point by choice. Off-design behavior would need a Mach sweep, which is listed as future work, not claimed.

Self-check
What is pi_NS(M) for M below 1?

1. A subsonic streamtube has no terminal shock.

Which cross-check must agree within 0.5 percent, and with what?

X1: recovery_throat times p0_inf against SU2's mass-flux-averaged total pressure on the same marker.

In the talk: S4, S22, B1. Record: docs/specs/00_decisions.md section 1.1 (an argument, no run record).

Definition

Near a wall the flow slows to zero in a thin boundary layer. A laminar layer is smooth and its thickness grows as sqrt(x). A turbulent layer mixes more, so it gives higher skin friction and higher heat flux. Transition from laminar to turbulent depends on the Reynolds number, the Mach number, the wall temperature and the roughness. The study uses the laminar Navier-Stokes equations.

Key relations
\[ \begin{aligned} Re_x &= \frac{\rho_e u_e x}{\mu_e}, \qquad \delta \approx \frac{5x}{Re_x^{1/2}} \quad \text{(Blasius, incompressible form)} \\ &\text{Transition indicator: } \frac{Re_\theta}{M_e} \quad \text{(sharp cold ramps: transition band 200 to 300)} \end{aligned} \]
Worked example (00_decisions.md section 1.1) \[ Re_L = \frac{Re}{\mathrm{m}} \times L = 1.506 \times 10^{6} \times (0.25 \text{ to } 0.33) \le 5 \times 10^{5} \quad (\approx 7 \times 10^{5} \text{ in post-shock edge units}) \]

Transition on sharp cold-wall ramps at Mach 3 to 4 occurs at \(Re_x = 10^{6}\) to \(3 \times 10^{6}\) in ground tests. At the end of ramp 1, \(Re_\theta / M_e \approx 100\), below the 200 to 300 band. This is an estimate, and the talk says so.

Interview questions
How sure are you that the flow is laminar?

It is a defensible modeling choice for a sub-scale body at 30 km, supported by the Reynolds numbers above. It is not measured. A full-scale vehicle at lower altitude would be turbulent, and backup slide B1 shows what a turbulent model changes.

Does a cold wall help or hurt transition?

A cold wall stabilizes the first instability mode and delays transition. It can destabilise the second (Mack) mode at higher Mach numbers, but at Mach 3 to 4 the net effect is a delay.

Self-check
How does laminar boundary-layer thickness scale with x?

As the square root of x.

Is the laminar claim backed by a run record?

No. It is an analytic estimate and the outline marks it as such.

Definition

Wall heat flux is the heat per unit area that enters the wall, q_w = k (dT/dn) at the wall. For a laminar flat plate the Eckert method evaluates the incompressible Blasius result at a reference temperature T* that accounts for compressibility and wall cooling. The study uses it to check the laminar wedge heating.

Key relations (00_decisions.md section 4, V2b)
\[ \begin{aligned} T^{*} &= \tfrac{1}{2}\left(T_e + T_w\right) + \tfrac{11}{50}\left(T_{aw} - T_e\right), \qquad T_{aw} = T_e\left(1 + r\,\tfrac{\gamma-1}{2} M_e^2\right), \quad r = Pr^{1/2} \\ St^{*} &= 0.332\, (Re^{*}_x)^{-1/2}\, Pr^{-2/3} \quad \text{(properties at } T^{*}) \\ q_w &= St^{*}\,\rho^{*} u_e\, c_p\left(T_{aw} - T_w\right) \end{aligned} \]
Worked example (report row V2b, development run)

On the laminar wedge, over \(0.3 \le x / X_w \le 0.9\):

\[ \overline{\left| \frac{q_{\mathrm{CFD}} - q_{\mathrm{Eckert}}}{q_{\mathrm{Eckert}}} \right|} = 1.60\ \%, \qquad \max \left| \frac{q_{\mathrm{CFD}} - q_{\mathrm{Eckert}}}{q_{\mathrm{Eckert}}} \right| = 1.95\ \% \]

against the hard tolerance of \(10\ \%\) mean and \(15\ \%\) maximum. Eckert against similarity solutions is itself good to about \(3\ \%\) at these conditions.

Interview questions
Why exclude the leading edge from the heating objective?

A sharp flat plate has a 1/sqrt(x) heating singularity. The computed peak there is set by the first cell size, not by the design. The wall-node temperature also departs from 300 K within about 30 mm of the tip (B2). The objective therefore starts on ramp 2 at x of 0.1 m.

What limits the accuracy of a wall heat flux in a finite-volume code?

The first-cell height, because q_w is a wall gradient. The mesh puts the first cell at 20 micrometers, which gives a wall-cell Reynolds number of about 20, and the grid study measures how much the peak moves between mesh levels.

Self-check
What is the recovery factor r for a laminar layer?

\(r = Pr^{1/2}\), about 0.85 for air.

What did V2b measure, and did it pass?

Mean 1.60 percent, maximum 1.95 percent against Eckert. Pass.

Definition

At a blunt-body stagnation point the heat flux scales with the square root of the velocity gradient at the edge of the boundary layer and with the enthalpy difference between the flow and the wall. Fay and Riddell gave the standard correlation. The velocity gradient comes from modified Newtonian theory, which uses the pitot pressure behind the bow shock.

Key relations (00_decisions.md section 4, V3; two-dimensional cylinder constant 0.57)
\[ \begin{aligned} q_{FR} &= 0.57\, Pr^{-3/5}\,(\rho_e \mu_e)^{2/5}\,(\rho_w \mu_w)^{1/10}\,\left(\frac{du_e}{dx}\right)^{1/2}\left(h_0 - h_w\right) \\ \frac{du_e}{dx} &= \frac{1}{R}\left(\frac{2\,(p_{02} - p_\infty)}{\rho_e}\right)^{1/2} \\ \text{Billig stand-off: } \frac{\delta}{R} &= 0.386\,\exp\!\left(\frac{4.67}{M^2}\right) \end{aligned} \]
Worked example (00_decisions.md and the report)

Cylinder \(R = 10\ \mathrm{mm}\):

\[ \begin{aligned} p_{02} &= 21.068\, p_\infty = 25\,219\ \mathrm{Pa}, \qquad \frac{du_e}{dx} = \frac{1}{R}\left(\frac{2\,(p_{02} - p_\infty)}{\rho_e}\right)^{1/2} = 7.213 \times 10^{4}\ \mathrm{s^{-1}} \\ h_0 - h_w &= c_p\,(951.3 - 300)\ \mathrm{J/kg}, \qquad q_{FR} = 2.443 \times 10^{5}\ \mathrm{W/m^2} \end{aligned} \] \[ \text{first numerics, fine mesh: } 7.74 \times 10^{5}\ \mathrm{W/m^2}\ (+216.84\ \%), \qquad \text{entropy fix } 0.3: 2.333 \times 10^{5}\ \mathrm{W/m^2}\ (-4.49\ \%),\ \text{medium to fine } -3.46\ \% \]

The medium mesh diverged with the first numerics. Hard tolerances: \(15\ \%\) on the fine mesh and \(5\ \%\) medium to fine.

Interview questions
Why did the first attempt fail by a factor of three?

Roe's scheme with a small entropy fix shows the carbuncle instability on a blunt body: a spurious bulge of the bow shock on the stagnation line that destroys the heating prediction. The six-scheme comparison and the split test showed that the entropy fix, not the CFL cap, is the cure.

Why is the Fay-Riddell tolerance as wide as 15 percent?

The correlation and the Newtonian velocity gradient together carry about 10 percent. A tighter tolerance would test the correlation, not the solver. The report also evaluates q_FR with the velocity gradient taken from the CFD edge velocity.

Self-check
How does stagnation heating scale with nose radius?

As \(R^{-1/2}\). A blunter nose gives less heating.

Which V3 check was not in the first report, and how was it closed?

The Billig stand-off check needs a volume output file, which the production runs do not save. A development run of the fine cylinder with volume output (2 November, runs/dev/2026-11-02_flow_fields) gives \(\delta/R = 0.546\) against Billig's \(0.517\), a deviation of \(+5.6\ \%\) inside the 15 percent report tolerance.

B. CFD numerics and validation

Textbook: Blazek, Computational Fluid Dynamics: Principles and Applications, chapters 4 to 6 (spatial discretization, upwind schemes, time stepping). Roache, "Perspective: a method for uniform reporting of grid refinement studies" (1994). Free: SU2 documentation (su2code.github.io), especially "Convective schemes", "Slope limiters" and "Linear solvers".

In the talk: S7, S21 row 1, B4. Record: docs/specs/00_decisions.md section 6, runs/dev/2026-11-01_numerics/README.md, runs/dev/2026-11-01_cylinder_v3_attempt2 and attempt3.

Definition

A finite-volume code stores the mean of the conserved variables in each cell and updates it from the fluxes through the cell faces. The convective flux at a face is an approximate solution of the Riemann problem between the two neighbor states. Roe's flux linearizes this problem. Near a sonic point Roe's flux can admit an expansion shock and on blunt bodies it can show the carbuncle. The entropy fix adds dissipation to the small eigenvalues and removes both. SU2's ENTROPY_FIX_COEFF sets how large that region is.

Key relation
\[ \begin{aligned} \mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{face}} &= \tfrac{1}{2}\left(\mathbf{F}_L + \mathbf{F}_R\right) - \tfrac{1}{2}\,\lvert \mathbf{A}_{\mathrm{Roe}} \rvert\left(\mathbf{U}_R - \mathbf{U}_L\right) \\ \lvert\lambda\rvert &\;\to\; \frac{\lambda^2 + \varepsilon^2}{2\varepsilon} \quad \text{when } \lvert\lambda\rvert \lt \varepsilon \quad \text{(Harten-type entropy fix)} \end{aligned} \]
Worked example (numerics and cylinder records)

Euler wedge, \(\log_{10}\) of the RMS density residual:

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{AUSM+-up2, CFL 5 fixed:} &\ \text{diverged at iteration 50} \\ \text{AUSM+-up2, adaptive CFL:} &\ -2.49 \to -2.48 \ \text{(stalled)} \\ \text{Roe, } \varepsilon = 0.1, \text{ adaptive CFL:} &\ -3.45 \to -12.05 \ \text{in 603 iterations} \end{aligned} \]

On the cylinder, Roe at \(\varepsilon = 0.1\) then failed Fay-Riddell by \(+216.84\ \%\). A six-scheme comparison and a split test showed that \(\varepsilon = 0.3\) is the cure and the CFL cap is not. Final setting: \(\varepsilon = 0.3\) for every family. On the laminar wedge the larger fix moved the Eckert deviation from \(0.75\ \%\) to \(1.6\ \%\), inside tolerance.

Interview questions
Does a larger entropy fix make the solution more diffusive?

Yes, slightly, in regions with small wave speeds. The wedge heating moved from 0.75 to 1.6 percent deviation. That cost was accepted because the alternative was a wrong stagnation heating, and the change is recorded with both numbers.

Why not use HLLC or AUSM, which are carbuncle-free?

They were in the six-scheme comparison on the coarse cylinder (attempt 2). Definition

A first-order scheme uses the cell mean at the face and smears shocks over many cells. MUSCL reconstructs a linear variation inside each cell from the gradient, which gives second-order accuracy in smooth regions. A limiter reduces the slope near a discontinuity so that no new extrema appear. A non-smooth limiter switches on and off from one iteration to the next, so the residual oscillates instead of falling. Smooth limiters such as Venkatakrishnan's reduce this at the cost of a little extra dissipation.

Second order in smooth regions by construction. The grid study does not measure an observed order because the changes between levels are too small and not monotone, and the report says "order undefined" rather than quoting one.

What does the limiter do at a shock?

It drives the slope toward zero, so the face value falls back to the first-order cell mean and no overshoot appears.

Definition

Definition

A Gaussian process is a prior over functions: any finite set of function values is jointly Gaussian with a mean and a covariance given by a kernel k(x, x'). Conditioning on observed data gives a posterior with a closed-form mean and variance at any new point. One GP is fitted per objective: y1 = recovery_total and y2 = log q_peak. Inputs are scaled to the unit square. The kernel is Matern 5/2 with one lengthscale per input (automatic relevance determination).

Key relations
\[ \begin{aligned} y &= c + f(\mathbf{x}) + \epsilon, \qquad f \sim \mathcal{GP}(0, k), \qquad \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_n^2) \\ k(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{x}') &= \sigma_f^2\left(1 + \sqrt{5}\,r + \tfrac{5}{3} r^2\right)\exp\!\left(-\sqrt{5}\,r\right), \qquad r^2 = \sum_{d} \frac{(x_d - x'_d)^2}{\ell_d^2} \\ \mu(\mathbf{x}_*) &= c + \mathbf{k}_*^{\mathsf T}\left(\mathbf{K} + \sigma_n^2 \mathbf{I}\right)^{-1}(\mathbf{y} - c) \\ \sigma^2(\mathbf{x}_*) &= k(\mathbf{x}_*, \mathbf{x}_*) - \mathbf{k}_*^{\mathsf T}\left(\mathbf{K} + \sigma_n^2 \mathbf{I}\right)^{-1}\mathbf{k}_* \quad (+\,\sigma_n^2 \text{ for a predictive interval}) \\ \log p(\mathbf{y}) &= -\tfrac{1}{2}(\mathbf{y}-c)^{\mathsf T}\left(\mathbf{K} + \sigma_n^2 \mathbf{I}\right)^{-1}(\mathbf{y}-c) - \tfrac{1}{2}\log\left\lvert \mathbf{K} + \sigma_n^2 \mathbf{I}\right\rvert - \tfrac{n}{2}\log 2\pi \end{aligned} \]
Worked example (hypers.json on the 92-point pool) \[ \begin{aligned} \text{recovery: } & \ell_1 = e^{2.30} = 10.0, \quad \ell_2 = e^{1.87} = 6.5 \text{ box widths}, \quad \sigma_n^2 = 1.1 \times 10^{-6} \ (\sigma_n \approx 4 \times 10^{-5} \text{ in recovery}), \quad \log p(\mathbf{y}) = 443 \\ \log q_{\mathrm{peak}}: & \ \ell_1 = 15.7, \quad \ell_2 = 5.7 \text{ box widths}, \quad \sigma_n^2 = 2.1 \times 10^{-6} \ (\approx 0.03\ \% \text{ in } q) \end{aligned} \]

Lengthscales of several box widths mean nearly linear surfaces, steeper in \(\theta_2\).

Interview questions
Why a GP and not a neural network?

Because on this problem it was measured. The same replay was run with a deep ensemble of five small networks (runs/replay/2026-11-02_mlp, settings fixed before the run): the GP is 2 to 10 times more accurate on held-out designs at every budget, worse for the ensemble in 10 of 10 seeds on identical training sets, and about a quarter of the cost per step. With two inputs and under fifty smooth samples the GP's prior is the right inductive bias, its intervals come from the posterior, and it needs no GPU. EHVI with the ensemble still finds the whole front by 32 runs, so the active-learning conclusion does not depend on the choice (backup slide B7).

Why Matern 5/2 and not the squared exponential?

Matern 5/2 is twice differentiable, which matches smooth but not analytic responses, and it is less prone to ill-conditioned covariance matrices than the squared exponential on near-linear data. It is the default choice in the Bayesian optimization literature.

q spans a factor of about 2 across the pool and relative errors matter, so the log makes the noise and the lengthscales uniform across the range.

Definition

Definition

Definition

An acquisition function scores every unselected candidate from the GP posterior and the best candidate is run next. EHVI is the expected increase of the hypervolume if the candidate were observed, taken over the posterior of both objectives. The code estimates it by Monte Carlo: 128 draws per candidate from the two independent Gaussians, the exact hypervolume improvement of each draw, then the mean. Max-variance picks the candidate with the largest predictive variance; it learns the whole box and ignores the front. Both are deterministic given the seed.

Key relation
\[ \begin{aligned} \mathrm{EHVI}(\mathbf{x}) &= \mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{HV}\!\left(\mathrm{front} \cup \{\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x})\}\right) - \mathrm{HV}(\mathrm{front})\right], \qquad f_j(\mathbf{x}) \sim \mathcal{N}\!\left(\mu_j(\mathbf{x}), \sigma_j^2(\mathbf{x})\right) \\ \mathrm{EHVI}(\mathbf{x}) &\approx \frac{1}{128}\sum_{k=1}^{128} \mathrm{HVI}\!\left(\mathbf{f}^{(k)}\right) \end{aligned} \]

Definition

A pool-based replay compares sampling strategies offline on a fixed set of completed runs. Each strategy may only "run" points from the pool, so all strategies share the same ground truth and no extra CFD is needed. Random picks uniformly among unused pool points. LHS draws a fresh maximin Latin hypercube for each budget and takes the nearest unused pool point to each sample. All four strategies start from the same 8 random points for a given seed, add one point per step to 48, over 10 seeds, and report at n in 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 32, 40, 48.

Key relation
\[ \begin{aligned} \mathrm{RMSE}_{\mathrm{recovery}} &= \left(\frac{1}{n_{\mathrm{held}}}\sum_i \left(\mu_i - y_i\right)^2\right)^{1/2} \\ \mathrm{RMSE}_{q,\mathrm{rel}} &= \left(\frac{1}{n_{\mathrm{held}}}\sum_i \left(e^{\,\mu_{\log,i} - y_{\log,i}} - 1\right)^2\right)^{1/2} \end{aligned} \]
Worked example (final against interim)

The interim replay on 67 points and the final on 92 gave the same verdicts. Two numbers moved: the budget at which EHVI overtakes random and LHS on error went from 32 to 40, and the true front grew from 22 to 26 points. The report keeps both runs and says which numbers changed.

Interview questions
Is a replay on 92 points a fair test of active learning?

It is a fair test of the ordering of strategies at small budgets, which is the question. Its limit is that candidates are restricted to the pool, so EHVI cannot pick a point between pool points. The confirmation run is the one online step that escapes this limit.

Why 10 seeds?

Enough to show the spread as an interquartile band and to say "10 of 10 seeds" for the zero-regret result; the whole replay then takes about 20 minutes on a CPU. More seeds would not change a verdict that is unanimous.

Nearest unused pool point.

The confirmation run passed. How much does one run prove?

In the talk: B8. Record: runs/profiles/2026-11-02/operator_compare.csv, docs/validation/2026-11-02_operator_profiles.md, runs/profiles/2026-11-02/pca_floor.csv, spec docs/specs/task4c_operator_profiles.md.

Definition

A scalar surrogate predicts one number per design. An operator model predicts a whole function per design. Here the function is the wall heat flux along ramp 2, on 128 points in the coordinate \(s \in [0, 1]\) of the objective window. The Fourier neural operator (FNO) lifts the inputs to 32 channels. Each of its four layers adds two terms: a pointwise linear map, and a convolution done in Fourier space on the lowest 16 modes only. The baseline projects the profiles onto their principal components and fits one GP to each coefficient. Both models predict \(\log q\). The scalar GP on \(\log q_{\mathrm{peak}}\) is the third model, for the peak only.

Key relations
\[ \begin{aligned} v_{l+1}(s) &= \sigma\!\left( W_l\, v_l(s) + \mathcal{F}^{-1}\!\left[ R_l(k)\, \mathcal{F}[v_l](k) \right]\!(s) \right), \qquad k \le 16 \\ \log q(s) &\approx \mu(s) + \sigma_y \sum_{j=1}^{K} c_j(\theta_1, \theta_2)\, v_j(s), \qquad K = \text{fewest components for } 99.9\ \% \text{ of variance} \\ \varepsilon_{L2} &= \frac{\lVert \hat{q} - q \rVert_2}{\lVert q \rVert_2} \end{aligned} \]
Worked example (operator_compare.csv, pca_floor.csv)

Data: 92 pool cases re-run on the container; all 92 match their Colab records (largest difference \(3.3 \times 10^{-16}\)). The window is 110 to 218 mm long, so the grid step is

\[ \Delta x = \frac{L}{127} = \frac{110\ \mathrm{mm}}{127} \text{ to } \frac{218\ \mathrm{mm}}{127} = 0.86 \text{ to } 1.72\ \mathrm{mm} \]

Medians over held-out designs, 5-fold cross-validation (all data):

\[ \begin{aligned} \varepsilon_{L2}: &\quad \text{FNO } 0.11\ \%, \quad \text{PCA + GP } 0.32\ \%, \quad \frac{0.32}{0.11} \approx 3 \\ \text{peak error}: &\quad \text{scalar GP } 0.013\ \%, \quad \text{FNO } 0.092\ \%, \quad \text{PCA + GP } 0.18\ \% \\ \text{points in the 90\% band}: &\quad \text{FNO } 0.93, \quad \text{PCA + GP } 0.11 \\ \text{fit time}: &\quad \text{FNO } 1030\ \mathrm{s}, \quad \text{PCA + GP } 4.7\ \mathrm{s} \end{aligned} \]

With 24 training runs: \(\varepsilon_{L2}\) is 0.15 percent (FNO) against 0.36 percent (PCA + GP). The 99.9 percent rule kept \(K = 3\). The post-hoc truncation floor, the best projection onto \(K\) training components, is

\[ K = 3:\ 0.32\ \%, \qquad K = 5:\ 0.12\ \%, \qquad K = 8:\ 0.056\ \% \]

The \(K = 3\) floor equals the PCA + GP error. So all of the baseline error is truncation, and its GPs are almost exact. A baseline with more components was not run, because the rule was fixed before the results.

Interview questions
The FNO won. Why does the design still use the scalar GP?

The design objective is the peak, and the scalar GP predicts the peak 7 times better than the FNO (median 0.013 against 0.092 percent). It models exactly the quantity asked; the operator spends its capacity on the whole curve. The operator adds where the peak sits and the shape of the load, which a scalar cannot give.

Is the FNO really better than PCA + GP?

Under the settings fixed in the spec, yes: about 3 times lower profile error, lower for 91 of 92 designs. But the baseline error equals its 3-component truncation floor, and the floor at 5 components equals the FNO error. So the margin comes from the 99.9 percent rule, not from a limit of GP regression. I did not re-run the baseline with more components after seeing the result; that would be tuning the comparison.

Self-check

Definition

The three-ramp inlet has six inputs: three ramp angles, and the flight Mach number, altitude and wall temperature. The designer chooses the angles. The flight condition is not chosen; it varies over an envelope. The low-fidelity model is Euler CFD for recovery and the Eckert flat-plate relation on the Euler wall state for heating. The high-fidelity model is laminar Navier-Stokes. The two-level model (Kennedy and O'Hagan) uses one GP for the cheap level and a second GP for the discrepancy. The feasibility model is a logistic regression that gives the probability that SU2 converges. The robust query asks for the angles with the best nominal recovery, with the heat flux under a cap at every envelope point.

Key relations
\[ \begin{aligned} y_H(\mathbf{x}) &= \rho\, y_L(\mathbf{x}) + \delta(\mathbf{x}), \qquad \delta \sim \mathcal{GP} \\ p(\mathbf{x}) &= \frac{1}{1 + e^{-(\mathbf{w}^{\top} \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}) + b)}}, \qquad \alpha(\mathbf{x}) = \mathrm{EHVI}(\mathbf{x})\, p(\mathbf{x}) \\ \max_{\theta_1, \theta_2, \theta_3} &\ \mu_{\eta}(\boldsymbol{\theta}, \mathbf{e}_{\mathrm{nom}}) \quad \text{subject to} \quad \min_{\mathbf{e} \in E} \Phi\!\left( \frac{\ln Q - \mu_{\ln q}(\boldsymbol{\theta}, \mathbf{e})}{\sigma_{\ln q}(\boldsymbol{\theta}, \mathbf{e})} \right) \ge 0.9 \end{aligned} \]
Worked example (task9_tables.md, feasibility.json, inverse/result.json)

Pools: 159 NS points, 152 converged and 7 failed; 316 Euler points, all converged. Two-level model against NS alone, median held-out error over 10 seeds:

\[ \begin{aligned} q_{\mathrm{peak}}: &\quad \text{two-level, 12 NS runs: } 0.025, \qquad \text{NS only, 48 runs: } 0.026 \;\Rightarrow\; \frac{48}{12} = 4 \text{ times fewer NS runs} \\ \eta: &\quad \text{two-level, 12 runs: } 0.0032, \qquad \text{NS only, 12 runs: } 0.0064, \qquad \text{NS only, 24 runs: } 0.0029 \\ \text{raw low fidelity}: &\quad q \text{ off by } 0.295, \quad \eta \text{ off by } 0.0047, \qquad \rho = 1.00 \text{ to } 1.07 \end{aligned} \]

Feasibility, 5-fold cross-validation: accuracy 0.950, but the rule "always converges" scores \(152/159 = 0.956\). The rank AUC is 0.63.

Robust query: the cap is \(Q = 16{,}081\ \mathrm{W/m^2}\), the 40th percentile of the pool. At Mach 4.5, 28 km and 300 K, the lowest predicted median over all angles is \(27{,}202\ \mathrm{W/m^2}\):

\[ \ln\frac{Q}{q} = \ln\frac{16{,}081}{27{,}202} = -0.526 \]

The held-out heating error is about 1 percent at 96 runs. A 69 percent excess is many standard deviations, so \(P(q \le Q)\) is close to zero. No angles meet the cap at this point, so the query has no answer.

Definition

The tools are plain Python functions underneath. MCP adds a typed boundary that any client can call, a log of every call with its arguments, and a place to put the gates so that no path around them exists. The protocol is a thin wrapper, not the substance.

Definition

A gate is a pure function of the parameters that refuses a design before a mesh exists. Each message names the gate, the value and the limit.

  • Gate 1, attached shocks: theta1 at most theta_max(M_inf) minus 2 degrees; theta2 at most theta_max(M1) minus 2 degrees.
  • Gate 2, shock-on-lip feasibility: the lip is at least 0.02 m downstream of the corner and below 0.6 of the domain height.
  • Gate 3, duct height at least 0.008 m.
  • Gate 4, total turning theta1 plus theta2 at most 24 degrees (26 at Task 0, trimmed on the 12/14 evidence).
  • Gate 5, wall-cell Reynolds number on ramp 2 at most 50.
Worked example (box diagnostics)

12/12, 8/14 and 5/14 converged. 12/14 diverged on both meshes and with both CFL caps. The gate was set at 24 degrees, which excluded 4 of the 96 Sobol points (indices 0, 40, 72, 80 in designs/pool_sobol96.csv). The 25 degree band was not tested and the slide says so.

Interview questions
Is gate 4 physics or a patch?

It is a design-space definition made on recorded evidence: the laminar solution at 26 degrees total turning does not reach a steady state. Whether the real flow separates there is not claimed. The gate keeps the pool inside the region where the model is trustworthy, and the front is stated to hold inside that region only.

Why a 2 degree margin on detachment?

theta_max is an inviscid value; the boundary layer displaces the ramp and the shock is slightly stronger. Two degrees keeps the shocks attached with margin without excluding useful designs: theta_max is 38.8 degrees at Mach 4 and the box ends at 12.

Definition

Three checksums protect the chain. The mesh fingerprint is a hash of the mesh the staging side built; the worker rebuilds the mesh from the parameters and must get the same fingerprint. The config hash covers the lines of case.cfg that SU2 reads, not the comment lines. The result digest is a sha256 the worker writes next to every result and error file; ingest refuses a text that does not match it.

Worked example (the two tooling failures)

Failure 5: the first config hash covered the whole file. One geometry number in a comment line differed in its last digit between the container and Colab (\(h_{\mathrm{duct}}\), \(3 \times 10^{-18}\)), so the worker refused pool job db92dcf68d, and later 63 jobs, as config mismatches. Interview questions
Why did the whole-file hash fail across machines?

The comment line prints the geometry at full floating-point precision, and the last digit of one value differed by 3e-18 between two machines. SU2 ignores comment lines, so the case was identical. The guard was stricter than the thing it guarded.

Is retyping base64 by an agent a real risk?

It happened: two files out of four were altered, and one mixed the fields of two records. The lesson on the slide is that any copy step without a checksum is a silent-corruption path, whoever performs it. The digest closed it, and the later error rate shows why it was needed.

In the talk: S11, B9. Record: worker/run_worker.py, docs/specs/01_interfaces.md, docs/transcripts/2026-11-02_worker_restart_and_retry.md, fault matrix row F8, docs/faults/real_fault_matrix.md.

Definition

Colab cannot accept inbound connections, so jobs travel through a shared Drive folder. A job is one JSON file in jobs/ with the parameters, the code commit, the config hash and the mesh fingerprint. The worker polls the folder every 20 seconds, writes flag files in runs/<id>/status/ (0-seen, 1-running, 2-done, 2-failed), rebuilds the mesh, checks the fingerprint and hash, runs SU2 with mpirun, and writes result.json with its digest. A heartbeat file is updated while a case runs. After a restart, a 1-running job whose heartbeat is older than 10 minutes is re-run once; finished jobs are never repeated.

Worked example (the transcript of 2 November)

The worker restarted at 01:46 UTC with --retry-reason config_mismatch, cleared 63 failed flags, and failed them again within four minutes because their job files still carried the old hash. The 63 jobs were re-staged as single files; the same worker picked them up from 04:01 without a second restart and finished them by 10:46. Failure reasons come from a fixed list: timeout, nonzero_exit, nan, postprocess_error, mesh_mismatch, config_mismatch.

Interview questions
What happens if Colab disconnects in the middle of a run?

The flag stays at 1-running and the heartbeat stops. The next worker sees a stale heartbeat, re-runs the job once and writes a fresh result. Finished runs have 2-done flags and are not touched. Fault F8 tests this with a killed process.

Why does the worker rebuild the mesh instead of downloading it?

The mesh is a few megabytes and deterministic from the parameters at a given commit. Rebuilding keeps the job file at 600 bytes and gives an independent check: if the fingerprints differ, the two sides are not running the same geometry and SU2 is not started.

What happens if the worker dies but SU2 keeps running?

SU2 runs in its own session, so that a timeout can kill its whole process group, and it survives a worker that dies alone. In the real-SU2 fault run (R5) a restarted worker then started a second SU2 in the same folder; the record was still right, because both runs were the same deterministic case, but two writers in one folder is unsafe. The worker now stops any process still running in the run folder before it starts a job (R5g: one solver at a time). A Colab runtime restart ends every process, so this needs the Python process to die alone, for example from an out-of-memory kill.

## Added after the run: rows not run, diagnosis of rows outside tolerance, caveats

Written by hand from the table and detail above; no number below is new except where marked arithmetic.

Rows of spec 7.1 not computed here, each with its reason:

- **Section polars at the design Reynolds number (SU2).** Not run: it needs the Colab worker, which was not used.
- **Propeller efficiency by blade element.** Not computed: no blade-element code exists in the repository. Only the actuator-disk route (App. L) was run.
- **Gust load factors.** Not computed: Anderson's chapter 6 has no gust load factor relation (reason in the row note above). No substitute was made.
- **Static margin by Anderson (8.76)-(8.78).** Not computed: `measurement/anderson.py` has no static-stability relation. The lattice-downwash static margin is in `2026-11-24_task12_stage3_aero_m250.md`, declared the same model, not independent (spec 7.1c item 3).
- **Range and endurance (Anderson (5.153), (5.169)).** No row of their own in 7.1a; they enter the take-off weight first estimate.
- **Engine rows.** In `2026-11-24_task12_stage3_engine_m250.md`.

Rows outside tolerance, not tuned:

- **Take-off power, -9.9 % against +-5 %.** Mattingly's (2.25)-(2.30) use k_TO 1.2 against Stage 1's 1.1 V_stall and a different transition arc (listed in the row note). The same row was -7.1 % on the TP100 pass. The take-off distance row (Anderson, +1.6 %, inside +-15 %) and the 450 hp rating against 130.8 hp required leave R7's take-off margin large either way.
- **Take-off weight, Anderson first estimate, +105 % against +-10 %.** The detail above gives the cause: the same route with Stage 1's own empty fraction 0.5348 returns W0 3,060.6 lb (-1.8 %), so the whole difference is Anderson's historical empty fraction 0.62 against Stage 1's built-up value. The build-up cross-check (+8.3 %, not judged) is numerically inside +-10 % but is Raymer's own method. The TP100 pass gave +87 % by the same cause. Whether 0.62 is a fair empty fraction for this aircraft is a question for the owner; it is not decided here.
- **Zero-lift drag, -41.1 % against +-10 %.** Anderson (2.37) with Cfe 0.00312 gives CD0 0.01462 against Stage 1's 0.02482; the Cfe that would reproduce Stage 1 is 0.0053. Stage 1's build-up includes engine, sensor and gear terms the equivalent-skin-friction method has no place for (row note). TP100 pass: -39 %.
- **Best-climb speed, -9.2 % against +-3 %.** Stage 1 searches from 1.2 V_stall upward and lands on that floor (71.0 kt); the closed form has no floor (64.5 kt). The sea-level rate of climb agrees to +0.24 %.
- **Propeller efficiency at climb, 0.62 against 0.80, difference -0.18 against +-0.05.** Full 450 hp at 70.7 kt with a 6.91 ft propeller gives 1,286 lb of thrust on 37.5 ft^2 of disk, 34 lb/ft^2, where the ideal efficiency is low; the real-to-ideal factor 0.85 then gives 0.62. Cruise (0.813, +0.013) and loiter (0.788, -0.012) are inside. Arithmetic, not a re-size: the sea-level climb power required scales as 1/eta_p, 182.3 hp x 0.80/0.62 = about 235 hp, still below the 450 hp rating, so the climb rate and the 1,000 ft/min requirement keep a large margin; the take-off thrust model (a static-thrust cap, a shared input) was not re-run with this efficiency.

Caveats carried: the M250 table is validated at sea level, static and 70-100 % power only; 73.1 % of the baseline's mission fuel burns in cells where App. K's choked-turbine assumption fails; R6 (maximum operating altitude, relight) rests on declared assumptions (ruling R-f); the 23,000 ft ceiling requirement is above the certified 20,000 ft B17 installations (`docs/validation/2026-11-24_task12_m250_r6_sources.md`). The rows that share the engine table (power at ceiling, ceiling, mission fuel, speeds that use it) agree with Stage 1 because the table is a shared input, not because it was verified.

## 1. What was asked

Four questions (spec section 1): the endurance-against-mass Pareto front for a 0.5 kg payload and
its drivers; whether active learning beats Latin hypercube and random sampling in six dimensions,
measured on a pool replay and against the exact front of the low-fidelity model; whether a
two-level model reaches a held-out error with fewer SU2 polars than SU2 alone; and the longest
endurance design under the 4.0 kg class C2 cap with P(mass <= cap) >= 0.9, confirmed by one SU2
polar. Every answer is reported whichever way it goes.

## 3. The SU2 airfoil family and what the gates caught

Recorded in the task ledger under decisions 6 and 7 and the mesh-pair blocks:

- The first smoke case (`runs/smoke/airfoil2d-96a9dd0f51`) was postprocessed with the compressible
  residual name and failed; the incompressible solver writes `rms[P]`, so `postprocess/history.py`
  falls back to it and records which column it read.
- At the 3,000-iteration budget the smoke case was still 10.5 % low in cl and 24.7 % high in cd
  against its own converged value (`runs/smoke2/airfoil2d-51d551f2cc`, 4,867 iterations). Decision
  6: budget 6,000 with SU2's Cauchy stop on lift and drag. Every airfoil run is still classified
  `not_converged` by the repository's 20 %-window drift rule, which measures the approach rather
  than the scatter; both numbers are kept and the aggregator keeps Cauchy-stopped runs with their
  drift as flags (`analysis/drone_polar.py`). Budget-exhausted runs are dropped (two of the six
  nominal angles, `runs/nominal/`, at 0 and 10 deg).
- The mesh fingerprint was rounded to 1 nm and the fine mesh did not reproduce across machines at
  identical quality digits; it is now 1 µm with the legacy value accepted
  (`geometry/structured.py`, `tests/test_fingerprint_tolerance.py`).
- Mesh pair on the nominal section at Re 2e5, alpha 6 (`runs/mesh/`, `runs/smoke2/`): coarse
  21,760 cells 8.3 min per angle, medium 43,008 cells 23 min, fine 84,152 cells 6,000 iterations
  without a stop; cl -0.47 % and cd +3.82 % coarse to medium, cl -1.19 % and cd +2.7 % medium to
  fine, with drifts on the fine run (4.4 % cl, 10.3 % cd) as large as the shifts. The table
  resolves the drift band, not the mesh (ledger block of 20:00 UTC).
- The E387 coordinate file needed one spline over the whole loop: a two-sided spline left a 29 deg
  kink at the nose (`geometry/airfoils.py`, `tests/task10/test_airfoil_file.py`).

## 4. Question 2: active learning against space-filling sampling

Two measurements, both on the low-fidelity model, both published.

**Pool replay** (`runs/replay_drone_pool/2026-11-08/replay.csv`, 192-point low pool, 10 seeds,
8 initial points): EHVI recovers the whole 15-point pool front by 32 evaluations (regret 0.000)
where random and LHS are at 0.118 and 0.107 after 40 and max-variance at 0.062. The easy case: a
smooth model, no live gates.

**Online, unrestricted candidates, gates live** (`runs/replay_drone_online/2026-11-08/online.csv`
and `2026-11-08_feas/online.csv`, regret against the exact front of the 9^6 grid,
`runs/drone_grid/2026-11-08/front.csv`, 249 points from 531,441 evaluations, 459,432 clean):

| n = 40 | random | lhs | al_ehvi | al_ehvi_feas | al_maxvar | al_maxvar_feas |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| regret | 0.294 | 0.271 | 0.317 | **0.092** | 0.353 | 0.268 |
| gated picks of 32 | 1.9 | 4.0 | 29.7 | 7.5 | 25.4 | 7.6 |

Plain EHVI **loses** to random and LHS because 30 of its 32 picks fail a gate: the front lies
against the gated edge of the box and the GPs know nothing about the gates. Weighting the
acquisition by a logistic feasibility model fitted on the evaluated points takes EHVI to a third of
LHS's regret; the same weighting lifts max-variance only to parity. The answer on this problem:
active learning beats space-filling sampling only when the acquisition knows where the design is
buildable.

## 6. Question 3: two-level against single-level — BUDGETS 8 TO 24 RECORDED (29 of 32 designs)

`analysis/mf_drone.py` fits the two-level model (every low-pool point as the low level, n SU2
designs as the high level) against a GP on the SU2 designs alone, held-out error against n. With
29 complete designs after pool batch 21 the budgets 8, 12, 16 and 24 form (each budget must leave
four designs held out); the record is `runs/drone_mf/2026-11-09/mf.csv`, ten seeds, overwritten in
place as the pool lands (the spec's dated blocks keep each reading). Mean over seeds:

| n SU2 designs | held out | endurance RMSE: raw low | SU2 only | two-level | two-level wins | 90 % coverage: SU2 only | two-level |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | 21 | 2.086 h | 1.061 h | **0.586 h** | 10 of 10 | 0.63 | 0.51 |
| 12 | 17 | 2.086 h | 0.944 h | **0.617 h** | 10 of 10 | 0.63 | 0.59 |
| 16 | 13 | 2.086 h | 0.750 h | **0.461 h** | 9 of 10 | 0.66 | 0.69 |
| 24 | 5 | 2.086 h | 0.805 h | **0.551 h** | 9 of 10 | 0.60 | 0.70 |

**What holds so far.** The two-level model is the better point prediction on the mean at every
budget, and SU2 alone does not reach the two-level error at 8 designs within 24 (its best is
0.750 h against 0.586 h). Neither model's 90 % interval covers 90 % of the held-out points at any
budget (0.51 to 0.70), so no interval from either may be quoted as a 90 % interval. Which model is
the less over-confident has changed back and forth as the pool grew (lower two-level coverage at
every budget at 22 designs, higher at the small budgets at 24 and 25, higher at the large budgets
at 29), so that comparison is not settled; the n = 24 row rests on five held-out designs per seed.
On MTOW the two-level relative RMSE is 0.0013 to 0.0015 against 0.0060 to 0.0401 for SU2 alone,
with the raw low model at 0.0023: mass is a low-fidelity quantity. The fitted endurance slope ρ
averages 0.83 to 0.89 per budget (single seeds 0.74 to 1.04). The quantity the correction absorbs is
known rather than assumed: the V6e bias of decision 8 (section 2) and the V6f discrepancy (lift
slope 19 to 23 % low, profile drag 43 to 52 % off), and the sign it takes (mean ρ < 1) matches the
endurance shortfall of SU2 against the low model on every complete design. The 32-design sweep is
the answer.

## 8. Biases that travel with every number

From the spec (section 13) and the record: no trim drag; fully turbulent skin friction on the
non-wing parts above Re 5e5; a fixed propulsive efficiency; the closed-form structure; one section
Reynolds number per design while the chord varies along the span; the low-fidelity section's
`section_lift_effectiveness` is `assumed` at 0.75 with band [0.6, 0.9] while the E387 tunnel
implies 0.93 to 0.97 (V6f; a ledger ruling, open); the high level's own V6e miss (decision 8); and
the Cauchy-stopped runs' drift (1 to 2 % in cl, 4 to 7 % in cd at Re 203,800). The one-at-a-time
sweep of the fixed quantities' bands at the inverse answer (`analysis/bias_sweep_drone.py`) is
PENDING on that answer; at the low-fidelity answer (`runs/drone_bias_lf/2026-11-09/`) the three
largest are skin areal density, propulsive efficiency and pack specific energy, each worth 13 to
17 % of endurance across its band.

**Coarse against medium (recorded 2026-11-09, `runs/pool_af/2026-11-09/bias.csv`):** ten pairs on
designs 0 to 3; medium over coarse cl 0.995 to 1.028, cd 0.945 to 1.016. The coarse pool's drag is a
few percent high in the working range; lift is mesh-converged to the drift band.

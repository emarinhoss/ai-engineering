# Task 12: VSPAERO cross-check of the M250 baseline (W/S 16, A 7)

An extra cross-check of lift slope, zero-lift CL, span efficiency, induced drag and pitch stiffness with NASA's VSPAERO vortex lattice, run on the OpenVSP model of the baseline. It is **not a row of the Stage 3 measurement table** and replaces none of them (`2026-11-24_task12_stage3_aero_m250.md` stays the record).

Numbers are from `runs/task12/openvsp/2026-11-24_m250_baseline_vspaero/results.json` (script `conceptual/vspaero_check.py`, commit `a9155f7` plus the version field noted below; OpenVSP 3.57.1, VSPAERO v.7.2.2, Python 3.12.3). Model: `runs/task12/openvsp/2026-11-24_m250_baseline/m250_baseline.vsp3`, built from `runs/task12/stage3/2026-11-24/m250_baseline_ws16_a7/P450/h1.05/design_pusher.json`.

## Set-up

- Solver: VSPAERO vortex lattice (thin surfaces, camber lines), inviscid lift and induced drag, compressibility-corrected (its lift slope rises about 1 % from Mach 0.158 to 0.227). Default wake settings (8 wake nodes, 5 wake iterations), not varied.
- Conditions, ISA 20,000 ft (speed of sound 1,036.9 ft/s), true airspeeds from the design's mission: **loiter** 97.04 kt, Mach 0.158; **cruise** (outbound) 139.49 kt, Mach 0.227. Lift slope and pitch stiffness are quoted at the loiter Mach; the cruise Mach gives about +1 % in CL_alpha and changes nothing else here (see `results.json`).
- Reference: S 194.81 ft^2, MAC 5.528 ft, span 36.93 ft (A = 7.00). Moments about x = 11.224 ft, the full-fuel centre of gravity (z = 0). Alpha -2 to 8 deg, 6 points; CL, CDi and Cm are fitted over the whole range.
- In the lattice: **wing** (alone); **tails** = wing + horizontal + vertical tail; **full** = tails + fuselage, which VSPAERO represents as two crossed flat plates along the axis. **Left out:** propeller (no slipstream), sensor turret, gear sponsons (thick bodies, no lifting surface). The solver's CDo column (flat-plate skin friction) is not used.
- Mesh levels: **L1** = 12 spanwise strips per section, 33 points around the airfoil (wing 352 panels, tails 896); **L2** = 24 strips, 65 points (wing 1,472 panels, tails 3,712). For the fuselage configuration two coarser levels S6 and S8 (6 and 8 strips) were added because L1 and L2 failed (below).
- **Limits of the model:** this is the conceptual OpenVSP model (render3d layout): wing section NACA 4415 and tails 0012 are display sections (Stage 1 fixes only 15 % thickness), no wing-body fairing, no inlet or exhaust, and the wing sits 0.35 ft below the fuselage axis. CL0, zero-lift angle and Cm0 come from that display camber (4 %), not from a design decision, so they are not design values. The lattice is inviscid: no stall, no viscous drag due to lift.

## Mesh sensitivity (L1 to L2, loiter Mach; the cruise Mach moves the same)

| Quantity | wing alone | wing + tails |
|---|---|---|
| CL_alpha | -1.1 % | -1.6 % |
| CL0 | +0.005 | +0.0045 |
| K from Trefftz plane (so e) | **-14 %** (e 1.004 to 1.166) | **-11 %** (e 1.028 to 1.155) |
| K from surface integration | +5.3 % (e 0.953 to 0.905) | +3.1 % (e 0.937 to 0.908) |
| Cm_alpha | -5.3 % (wing alone, positive) | magnitude +3.4 % (more stable) |
| neutral point | +0.06 ft (static margin +0.011 MAC) | +0.044 ft (static margin +0.008 MAC) |

- **Lift slope, CL0, neutral point and static margin are converged to about 1 to 2 %, 0.005 and 0.01 MAC.** The panel count rose four-fold.
- **Induced drag is not converged, and the two ways of taking it move in opposite directions.** At L2 the Trefftz-plane e is 1.17 and 1.16, above 1; for a nearly planar wing that is not physical, so the Trefftz value at L2 is a numerical artefact and the L1 value (1.00, 1.03) is not known to be right either. The near-field value falls from 0.95 to 0.91. I did not vary the wake settings or find the cause. The honest statement is **e between 0.90 and 1.17 from this run, so no better than the study's lattice (0.991) and not an independent confirmation of it.** The one firm thing is the ordering: every VSPAERO value of the inviscid e is above Raymer's 0.839, which is a whole-aircraft value that includes separation drag, so that is consistent.
- **The fuselage-plate configuration is not mesh-robust at all.** L1 (12 strips): CL(alpha) not a line (residual 0.05 to 0.06), slope 3.4 to 4.1; L2 (24 strips): diverged (CL of order 30 in the history, fitted slopes -39 and -157); both rejected by the validity gate and kept in `results.json`. The two coarser levels give plausible polars that disagree: S6 to S8 changes CL0 by +0.048, shrinks the magnitude of Cm_alpha by 41 %, moves the neutral point by -0.34 ft and the static margin by -0.060 MAC. A scratch probe (not in the record) at 16 strips with the fuselage gave nonsense too. I think the cause is the fuselage plate lying only 0.35 ft above the wing root, about the size of a panel, and the vertical plate overlapping the fin root, but I did not test that. **The fuselage-plate numbers are shown for completeness and should not be used; the aircraft-level comparison rests on the wing + tails configuration.**

## Differences, by method

- **Lift slope.** Thin-wing lattices and Helmbold/Raymer (12.6) with airfoil efficiency 1 are the same physics for a planar wing, and VSPAERO is -1.7 % (L1) / -2.7 % (L2) from Raymer at the same Mach. VSPAERO is 1.7 to 2.8 % above the study's lattice, which runs at Mach 0; about 0.9 % of that is the Mach number (Raymer's formula, 4.739 to 4.783). VSPAERO uses the camber line (4 % camber) rather than a flat plate; lift slope barely depends on that. The 9 to 10 % gap to the **installed** 5.155 is the fuselage factor F (about 1.28 on the whole wing) and the exposed-area ratio in Raymer's (12.9), which a wing alone does not carry. With fuselage plates the aircraft slope is only 1.7 to 2.1 % above the tails configuration at S6 and S8, whereas Raymer's F and exposed-area ratio together make the installed slope 12 % above the bare wing at the same airfoil efficiency. I cannot say which is right: the plates are a poor body model and not converged.
- **Aircraft slope.** VSPAERO's tails configuration is 3.5 to 5 % below Raymer's sum of installed wing and tail terms. Part of that is the fuselage lift that Raymer's installed wing slope includes and the tails model lacks (previous point); I did not decompose the rest.
- **e.** Inviscid VLM against an empirical whole-aircraft fit: not the same quantity, so the difference is not an error in either. Treated as K, the lattice-implied viscous drag due to lift is 0.004 to 0.015 depending on the lattice and its mesh, containing the study's 0.0083.
- **Pitch.** VSPAERO's tails neutral point is 0.24 to 0.28 ft aft of Raymer's (static margin +0.044 to +0.052 MAC). About half of that (0.13 ft, 0.023 MAC) is simply that the tails model has no fuselage, whose moment term (Cma_fus, destabilising) is in Raymer's (16.9) and is missing here; the remaining +0.11 to +0.15 ft (+0.020 to +0.028 MAC) is the wing and tail contributions, which includes the tail's downwash and lift slope. Against R13's band of 5 to 15 % MAC at full fuel: Stage 1 (0.116) and the study's lattice (0.116) are inside it; **VSPAERO's tails configuration gives 0.160 and 0.168, above the 15 % upper end, but it has no fuselage, whose moment lowers the margin; the fuselage-plate runs (not reliable) give 0.086 and 0.147.** So VSPAERO does not confirm that the margin is inside the band and does not show that it is outside; the question is open at about 0.03 MAC. The study's lattice margin (0.116) is Raymer's equation with only the downwash replaced, so it and Stage 1 are one model, not two.

## What this does and does not show

- Agreement: wing lift slope within 1.7 to 2.8 % of the study's lattice and within 2.7 % of Raymer's bare-planform value; aircraft lift slope within 5 % of Raymer; tails-configuration neutral point 0.24 to 0.28 ft (about 5 % MAC) aft of Raymer's, wing-alone aerodynamic centre within 3 % MAC of the record's. These are checks of the same kind of physics by a second code and nothing was tuned to agree. The model, conditions and L1/L2 levels of the final run were fixed before it; before it, scratch probes at 6, 8, 12 and 16 wing strips were used to diagnose the fuselage failure, and the S6/S8 levels and the validity gate were added as a result. Those probes are not in the record; the S6 and S8 cases in it are the same meshes re-run by the script.
- No agreement claimed: the induced drag factor (not converged), anything with the fuselage plates, CL0 (no counterpart), and compressibility beyond what Prandtl-Glauert gives.
- Conceptual-model limits: display airfoils and layout, inviscid thin surfaces, no propeller slipstream, sponsons or turret, no fuselage in the accepted numbers.
- The Stage 3 aero rows were not changed or re-run for this check.

## Files and reproduction

- Run folder: `runs/task12/openvsp/2026-11-24_m250_baseline_vspaero/`: for each of the eight cases the OpenVSP file with the tessellation (`<config>_<level>.vsp3`), VSPAERO's `.polar`, `.history`, `.log`, `.vspaero` input and small control files, and `results.json` (inputs, commit, versions, command, every polar and fit, validity flags and reasons, mesh sensitivity, Raymer's values). The bulk solution, mesh and load files (`.adb`, `.dat`, `.slc`, `.vspgeom`, `.lod`) are deleted by the script after the panel count is read.
- Command: `<Python 3.12 with OpenVSP> -m conceptual.vspaero_check --design runs/task12/stage3/2026-11-24/m250_baseline_ws16_a7/P450/h1.05/design_pusher.json --model runs/task12/openvsp/2026-11-24_m250_baseline/m250_baseline.vsp3 --out runs/task12/openvsp/2026-11-24_m250_baseline_vspaero`. Takes about 18 minutes on 4 cores, most of it the diverging fuselage case at L2.
- `vspaero_version` in `results.json` was added by hand from the `wing_L1.log` header after the run (the script gained the field afterwards); the binary is `/opt/OpenVSP/vspaero`, byte-identical to the copy the API calls.
- Tests: `tests/task12/test_vspaero_check.py` covers the pure functions (polar parser, lift and drag fits, neutral point, validity gate) with plain numbers; nothing in the suite needs OpenVSP.

## Setup

- Pool: the 92 pool runs in `runs/pool/`, all with status converged (the complete 96-point Sobol design
  minus the 4 points refused by gate 4).
- Ground truth: `recovery_total` and `q_peak_W_m2` from each record. Objectives in minimisation form are
  `-recovery_total` and `log q_peak`. The true pool front has 26 non-dominated points of 92.
- Strategies: `random`, `lhs` (fresh maximin Latin hypercube per budget, nearest unused pool point),
  `al_ehvi` (Monte-Carlo expected hypervolume improvement), `al_maxvar` (largest predictive variance). All
  start from the same 8 random points for a given seed, 10 seeds (0 to 9), one point per step to n = 48.
- Metrics on the held-out pool points at n in {8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 32, 40, 48}: RMSE of recovery (absolute),
  relative RMSE of q_peak, normalised hypervolume regret, interval coverage at 50/80/90/95 percent.
  At n = 48 the held-out set has 44 points, so one point is 2.3 percent of coverage.
- GP: Matern 5/2 with ARD, one GP per objective, fitted by marginal likelihood at every step of the
  active strategies.

Figures: `docs/validation/2026-11-02_replay_full_error_vs_n.png` (median and interquartile band over
seeds) and `docs/validation/2026-11-02_replay_full_calibration.png` (coverage at n = 48).

## Findings

1. Hypervolume regret (the quantity EHVI is built to reduce): `al_ehvi` is best at every budget. Its
   median regret is 0.081 at n = 12 against 0.141 (random), 0.117 (lhs) and 0.117 (al_maxvar); 0.013 at
   n = 24 against 0.073, 0.059 and 0.038; and exactly 0 in 10 of 10 seeds at n = 32, 40 and 48, meaning
   the 32 points EHVI selects always contain the whole 26-point true front. At n = 48 the others are still
   at 0.030 (random), 0.025 (lhs) and 0.008 (al_maxvar), and no seed of any other strategy reaches 0.
2. Held-out error (global accuracy): `al_ehvi` is the worst strategy on RMSE of recovery from n = 12 to
   n = 32 (2.9e-4 at n = 20 and 1.8e-4 at n = 32, against 1.0e-4 and 6.1e-5 for random, 6.5e-5 and
   3.9e-5 for lhs, 5.9e-5 and 2.2e-5 for al_maxvar), with the widest spread over seeds. It drops to
   2.6e-5 at n = 40 and 1.6e-5 at n = 48, overtaking random and lhs at n = 40 and ending close to
   al_maxvar (1.4e-5). On relative RMSE of q_peak it tracks random until n = 32 (6.2e-4 at n = 32 against
   5.8e-4 random, 4.0e-4 lhs and al_maxvar) and ends at 4.0e-4 at n = 48 against 4.5e-4, 3.9e-4 and
   3.6e-4. This is the expected trade-off, and on the full pool it is sharper than it looked on 67 points:
   EHVI spends its first 24 selections on the front, which on this box is the gate-4 edge and the low
   corner, and learns the interior last. `al_maxvar` is the best global learner at every budget, and
   `lhs` beats `random` throughout.
3. Scale of the errors: at n = 48 every strategy predicts recovery to 1.4e-5 to 3.1e-5 absolute (the
   pool spans 0.256 to 0.406) and q_peak to 0.036 to 0.045 percent relative. The response surfaces are
   very smooth over this box (lengthscales of several box widths, `runs/analysis/2026-11-02/hypers.json`),
   so the surrogate problem is easy and the gain of any strategy over random is a factor of about two in
   error at fixed n, not an order of magnitude.
4. Calibration at n = 48 (44 held-out points): the recovery intervals are too wide. Median empirical
   coverage is 0.83 to 0.98 at the 50 percent level, 0.98 to 1.0 at 80 percent and 1.0 at 90 and 95
   percent for all four strategies, above the 97 percent edge of the C1 flag band. For q_peak the
   intervals are close to nominal: 0.65 to 0.77 at the 50 percent level, 0.81 to 0.88 at 80 percent,
   0.84 to 0.91 at 90 percent and 0.89 to 0.92 at 95 percent, inside the 80 to 97 percent band for every
   strategy (EHVI is the lowest, 0.84 at the 90 percent level). The over-coverage on recovery is the
   conservative direction (errors smaller than the model says) and is reported as a finding, not
   refitted. With a fitted noise of about 4e-5 in recovery (Task 5 fit) and held-out errors of 1e-5 to
   3e-5, the predictive interval is dominated by the noise term, which is larger than the interpolation
   error at most held-out points; that is the mechanism, and no hyperparameter was changed.
5. Cost: one EHVI step (two GP fits plus the acquisition over the unselected pool) takes 1.7 s on the
   container CPU at n = 48, max-variance 1.5 s, lhs 0.15 s; random takes no fitting time between report
   budgets.

## Verdict against the PLAN gate

Active learning with EHVI beats random and LHS on the metric it targets (hypervolume regret) at every
budget, and from n = 32 it finds the complete true front in every seed while no other strategy does so
by n = 48. It does not beat them on global held-out error below n = 40, and the max-variance rule is the
better choice when the goal is a globally accurate surrogate. Both statements go on the slide. The
interval calibration is inside the C1 band for q_peak and conservative (flagged, above 97 percent
coverage at the 90 percent level) for recovery.

## Final against interim (67 points)

The verdicts did not change; two numbers moved. (a) The budget at which EHVI overtakes random and lhs
on held-out error moved from n = 32 to n = 40, and its recovery RMSE at n = 24 to 32 is larger than on
the interim pool (1.8e-4 against 1.4e-5 at n = 32), because the 25 late-arriving points were mostly
interior points EHVI had not sampled. (b) At n = 48 the recovery RMSE ranking is al_maxvar, al_ehvi,
lhs, random on both pools (1.4e-5, 1.6e-5, 2.3e-5, 3.1e-5 final against 1.4e-5, 1.3e-5, 2.3e-5, 4.8e-5
interim). The zero-regret result for EHVI from n = 32 holds on both pools (10 of 10 seeds), with the true
front growing from 22 to 26 points. Recovery coverage went from 0.95 to 1.0 to 1.0 at the 90 percent
level; q_peak coverage stayed inside the band.

## Carried into Task 5

The recovery intervals are conservative, so the 90 percent band of the recovery GP on the Pareto plot
and the recovery interval of the inverse query (`docs/validation/2026-11-02_pareto_inverse.md`) are
likely wider than needed; the q_peak intervals are about right.

## Question 1: active learning against LHS and random in six dimensions

`python -m analysis.replay3r` (offline replay on the NS pool; candidates include the failed points, a
failed pick spends a run; 12 shared random starting points per seed; 10 seeds).

Medians over 10 seeds:

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 24 | hypervolume regret | 0.246 | 0.292 | 0.047 | 0.000 | 0.000 |
| 96 | hypervolume regret | 0.057 | 0.113 | 0.000 | 0.000 | 0.000 |
| 48 | RMSE recovery | 0.0017 | 0.0017 | 0.0015 | 0.0017 | 0.0017 |
| 96 | RMSE recovery | 0.0014 | 0.0013 | 0.0008 | 0.0008 | 0.0008 |

| 96 | relative RMSE q_peak | 0.013 | 0.014 | 0.008 | 0.007 | 0.007 |
| 96 | failed runs among the picks | 4 | 4 | 4.5 | 4 | 4 |

- **Finding the front:** EHVI reaches zero hypervolume regret (median) by 24 runs. Random sampling
  still has 0.057 at 96 runs, and LHS has 0.113. This is a much larger win than in the two-ramp
  replay. The reason has to be stated with it: the pool's Pareto front of (recovery, q_peak) has
  only 7 points. All of them sit at the low-Mach end of the envelope (Mach 3.50 to 3.71), most at
  high altitude or a hot wall. The front is mostly a front over flight conditions, which a targeted
  search finds quickly, and not a front over ramp angles.
- **Surrogate accuracy:** in the first 24 to 48 runs the five strategies are within each other's
  spread. From 80 runs on, the three active strategies' held-out error is about 0.55 to 0.65 times
  that of random and LHS: 0.0008 against 0.0013–0.0014 in recovery at 96 runs, and 0.007–0.008
  against 0.013–0.014 in relative q error. Caveat, as in Task 4: the held-out set is the pool points not chosen, so it
  differs between strategies.
- **The feasibility factor changed nothing measurable.** EHVI with and without it picked differently
  in 72 of the 480 (seed, budget, metric) cells. From 48 runs on the medians match; at 16 to 32 runs
  they differ in both directions (RMSE recovery 0.0059 against 0.0068 at 16 runs; relative q error
  0.028 against 0.027 at 32 runs). Both made 4 failed picks by 96 runs (median). This follows from question 3: the classifier cannot tell failing points
  from converging ones.
- **Calibration:** the 90 percent intervals cover 0.78 to 0.89 of held-out points at 96 runs, and
  less at small n, so they are somewhat too narrow.

The raw low-fidelity value, with no NS run, has RMSE 0.0047 in recovery (Euler is close) and 0.295
relative error in q_peak (Eckert on the Euler wall state is off by about 30 percent, but its
correlation with NS is strong: fitted rho is 1.00 to 1.07).

- **Heating (q_peak):** yes, by a factor of about 4 in NS runs. The two-level model at 12 NS runs
  (median 0.025) matches NS-only at 48 runs (0.026). At every budget its error is between 0.1 and 0.6
  times the NS-only error.
- **Recovery:** yes, but only for small budgets. The two-level model at 12 runs (0.0032) matches
  NS-only at between 16 and 24 runs (0.0040 and 0.0029). From 24 to 32 runs on, the two are within each other's spread.
  Euler already captures most of the recovery physics, so NS adds little.
- **Intervals:** both models under-cover at small n. The two-level model covers 0.52 to 0.58 of
  held-out points at 12 runs, for nominal 0.90. From 48 runs on, both cover 0.79 to 0.89. The
  two-level model's errors are smaller than its intervals suggest at small n, so its uncertainty
  should not be trusted there.

## Question 3: can a classifier learn where the solver converges?

`python -m analysis.feas3r` (logistic regression, six inputs plus total turning, 5-fold stratified
cross-validation on the 159 NS points with 7 failures).

**No.**

- Cross-validated accuracy is 0.950. Always predicting "converges" gives 0.956.
- The rank AUC is 0.63 (0.5 is no skill).
- At a threshold of 0.5 it would have skipped none of the 7 failures and lost 1 good run.
- At 0.9 it would have skipped 3 failures and lost 14 good runs.

Five of the seven failures are below Mach 3.8, so there is a weak trend with Mach, but 7 failures in
159 is too few for six inputs. With the diagnosed failure being an outflow-boundary problem, the
classifier is learning where this solver set-up breaks, not where the inlet stops working.

## Question 4: robust inverse query

`python -m analysis.inverse3r`: maximize predicted nominal recovery over the three angles, subject to
P(q_peak <= cap) >= 0.9 at all 125 points of the 5 x 5 x 5 envelope grid.

- The cap is 16,081 W/m², fixed before the answer as the 40th percentile of q_peak over the 152
  converged NS runs.
- **Answer: no design meets it.** Among 4043 candidate angle sets (total turning ≤ 27°), the best
  minimum probability over the envelope is about 1e-147.
- At 103 of the 125 envelope points some design meets the cap. At the other 22 none does. These are
  the high-Mach, low-altitude, cold-wall points. The worst is Mach 4.5, 28 km, 300 K, where the
  lowest predicted q median over all candidates is 27,202 W/m², 1.7 times the cap. The per-point
  table is in the tables file.
- The query as specified mixes a pool-wide statistic (the cap) with a worst case over the envelope.
  That combination cannot be met: flight condition moves q_peak much more than the ramp angles do.
- No confirmation run was made, because there is no design to confirm.
